# Registro por grado: de lo infantil a lo juvenil (3° a 6°)

Used by the `crear-guia` and `crear-material` skills (and by `planeacion` to propose hilos).

Grades 3–6 (ages ≈ 8–12) span the move from childhood to early adolescence. Every material for these grades — guía, clase, quiz, taller, tarea — must fit the grade's maturity **and** move students one step toward the next stage: never babyish for a 6th grader, never too dense for a 3rd grader. Grades 7–11 use the juvenil register by default.

| | 3° (≈ 8–9 años) | 4° (≈ 9–10) | 5° (≈ 10–11) | 6° (≈ 11–12) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hilo conductor** | Fábulas, cuentos clásicos, animales, un personaje guía que explora | Aventuras y viajes (exploradores, *La vuelta al mundo en 80 días* contada), historias de inventos | *Las crónicas de Narnia* (*El león, la bruja y el ropero*), *Harry Potter* (libros 1–2), *El principito*, mitos y leyendas colombianas | *El hobbit*, *Percy Jackson*, civilizaciones (Egipto, Grecia, mayas, muiscas), vida de científicos |
| **Contextos de los ejemplos** | Recreo, tienda escolar, mascotas, juguetes, el salón | Casa, barrio, deportes, cocina, mapas del colegio | Ciudad (Cali), viajes, naturaleza, juegos de mesa, dinero en pesos | Tecnología, deportes, arte, construcción, medio ambiente, videojuegos (sin violencia) |
| **Lenguaje** | Oraciones cortas, trato cercano, cada palabra nueva con dibujo o ejemplo | Frases más largas; términos geométricos con definición sencilla | Vocabulario matemático formal, introducido y explicado | Registro juvenil y respetuoso; definiciones formales y notación |
| **Científicos / marco teórico** | Anécdotas ("¿Sabías que…?"), sin citas textuales | Anécdotas breves; una cita muy corta si es sencilla | Citas cortas explicadas con palabras propias | Citas textuales con fuente, como en grados superiores |
| **Tipo de trabajo** | Manipulativo, dibujar, contar, juego | Concreto → dibujo; primeros procedimientos escritos | Procedimientos completos, problemas de varios pasos | Justificar, generalizar, primeras fórmulas |

Rules:
- **Each grade is more mature than the one before — this is the main rule.** A 4th grader's interests are noticeably less childish than a 3rd grader's, a 5th grader's less than a 4th grader's, and so on. Materials for consecutive grades must feel different: don't reuse a lower grade's stories, characters or contexts in a higher grade; if the same topic or story appears again (e.g. symmetry in 4° and 5°), treat it with the older grade's register, examples and depth.
- **A smaller transition also happens within the year**: periodo I of a grade can lean slightly toward the previous column and periodo III toward the next one (more text, less play, more abstraction). This is secondary to the grade-to-grade difference.
- **Content must be age-appropriate**: no violence, fear, romance, alcohol, dieting/body weight, debts or adult economic stress; in literary threads choose only the gentle passages (Narnia's wardrobe and Tumnus, not battles; Harry Potter's Hogwarts classes and sorting, not deaths). *El señor de los anillos* and the later Harry Potter books are for grade 7 and up.
- **Don't infantilize the older grades**: no diminutives, "¡Hola, pequeño!", cartoonish exclamations or animal mascots in 5° and 6°.
- Numbers and quantities must be realistic for the age and for Colombia (prices in pesos, distances in Cali, school situations).
- If the requested story or example doesn't fit the grade (e.g. *El señor de los anillos* for 3°), say so and propose an age-appropriate alternative instead of adapting it silently.
- The mathematical level is still set by the grade's DBA; this table governs tone, stories and contexts, not content.
